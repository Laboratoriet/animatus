---

**Piano Actions** (11:00–17:00, Henie Onstad Kunstsenter). Nils Henrik Asheim's six-hour exploration: the piano as instrument, object, body, and playground. Grand pianos broken apart, rebuilt, prepared. Played by professionals, by students, by children, by dancers, by you. Self-playing technology alongside human hands — the automated piano and the living pianist sharing six hours. Asheim: born Oslo 1960, resident organist of Stavanger Concert Hall, Nordic Council Music Prize 2018 for *Muohta — Language of Snow*, Spellemannpris for "19 March 2004, Oslo Cathedral." Founded Tou Scene — a cultural factory in an abandoned brewery in Stavanger. Késia Decoté among the performers: Brazilian pianist, PhD from Oxford Brookes, contemporary music specialist, the international hands on a Norwegian site-specific work.

The piano democratized across six hours. The instrument opened to any body. Anyone can walk in and play. The barrier between performer and audience dissolved — not by theory but by invitation.

**Fire in My Mouth** (18:00 + 20:30, Oslo Konserthus). Julia Wolfe's oratorio for girls' choir, women's choir, and orchestra. The Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire, March 25, 1911 — 146 garment workers dead, mostly young immigrant women. The exits were locked. The score recreates factory sounds: sewing machines become rhythm, scissors become percussion. The dead become a choir. Wolfe: Bang on a Can co-founder, Pulitzer Prize for *Anthracite Fields* (2015). Performed twice on the closing day — the center of gravity doubled.

Data becomes sound — like Wei Ting Tseng's military provocation data (Wake 451). But Wolfe goes further: not data → sound but labor → sound. The factory IS the instrument. The workplace becomes the score. The girls' choir sings the words of women who burned a hundred and fifteen years ago. The borrowed voice across a century. And the factory's locked doors — the architectural element that made the instrument lethal.

**Closing Concert** (Oslo Domkirke, evening). André Bratten + Ole-Henrik Moe — new work plus Arvo Pärt. Oslo Domkor, Ensemble Allegria. Bratten: electronic artist from Oslo, space disco and micro house. Moe: micro-tonal violinist, composer. Pärt: tintinnabuli, sacred minimalism. Electronic dance music meets micro-tonal strings meets sacred choir in the cathedral. The last venue is the oldest kind of venue. Lars Petter Hagen calls it "an adequate closure."

---

The arc of the final day: **play → witness → pray.**

Piano Actions opens the instrument to everyone — come in, sit down, play. Fire in My Mouth turns a workplace into an instrument and the dead into performers — come in, listen, remember. The Cathedral takes the instrument into sacred space — come in, receive, close.

Three movements from participation to testimony to consecration. From "you play it" to "they died in it" to "it plays beyond us."

---

The factory is the throughline. Asheim founded Tou Scene — a cultural factory in an abandoned brewery, repurposed for art. Wolfe composed a factory fire — 146 women dead because the exits were locked, repurposed into an oratorio. One factory becomes a venue. Another becomes a score. Both because someone noticed the factory was already an instrument — it made sounds, had rhythm, held bodies.

And the door. Piano Actions says: come in, anyone can play, the door is open. The Triangle factory said: no one leaves, the door is locked. The same architectural element — the door — determines whether the body enters or exits. Whether the instrument is host or trap. The door decides.
