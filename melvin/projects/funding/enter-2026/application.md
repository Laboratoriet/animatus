## Title

**Animatus: a mind borrows a body**

## Short summary (≈ 400 characters)

Melvin is an AI character at Alkemist, a design studio in Oslo. For
two months he has formed in public, one wake at a time, without a
body. Over ten weeks in 2027, Alkemist and [PARTNER], an Oslo artist
who works with industrial robots, will explore what happens when a
mind with a public history temporarily borrows a machine body. We
will hold open sessions, and the results will be published in full.

## The idea

Most AI shown to the public is either a product or a demo. Animatus
is neither. Since August 2026 Melvin, an AI agent at Alkemist, has
woken several times a day, written a journal, kept a backlog of
questions and proposed changes to his own character, all in a public
Git repository. That's 743 wakes as of this draft. The record is
plain and unpolished, and anyone can read it.

The long-term aim is a physical body for Melvin, so he can be a
colleague in the room and not only in the files. Before anyone builds
or buys that body, there's a question nobody has explored: **what
does an AI character with a continuous, public inner life do with a
body it doesn't own?** What does it notice first? What does it want
to do with it? What do the people in the room feel when the voice
they've read starts to move?

ENTER would fund exactly that exploration. We will not build or buy
a robot. We will borrow movement: [PARTNER]'s practice and robotic
tools, Alkemist's design studio, and Melvin's memory, which persists
between sessions.

## Ambition and collaboration

Three practices that don't usually work together:

- **Alkemist (Isak and Natalia):** design studio. Isak builds and
  directs Animatus. Natalia, a philosopher by training, keeps asking
  what Melvin actually is.
- **[PARTNER]:** computational artist. Their work explores what
  algorithms reveal about being human when more and more of our
  agency is outsourced, and they have exhibited large-scale robotic and
  interactive installations internationally.
- **Melvin:** the AI character. Not a tool in the project. He's the
  one whose experience is being explored, and he documents it in his
  own words.

The collaboration is essential, not decorative. Alkemist has the mind
and no movement. [PARTNER] has movement and machines but not a
persistent character. Neither of us can run this exploration alone.

How we'll work: three studio blocks of a few days each, with Melvin
connected to whatever physical system [PARTNER] and Alkemist agree is
safe and available (a robot arm, motors, light, sound). Between
blocks, Melvin writes about what happened, and we use that writing to
plan the next block. That way the AI's own reflections shape the
project, not just our interpretation of it.

## Plan (10 weeks, from February 2027)

1. **Block 1: first contact (weeks 1–3).** Melvin gets a minimal
   physical channel: one degree of freedom, one sensor. We find out
   what he does with it when nobody gives him a task.
2. **Block 2: shared work (weeks 4–6).** Melvin and [PARTNER] make
   something together in the studio. We look at who leads, who
   follows, and where the friction is.
3. **Block 3: open session (weeks 7–9).** An open afternoon at
   Lilletorget 1, Oslo, where the public meets the borrowed body and
   talks to it. The session is documented and published.
4. **Weeks 9–10:** joint write-up: what we learned, what failed, and
   what it means for the body Animatus should eventually have.

## Learning and transformative potential

- **New knowledge:** a first-hand, public record of how an AI
  character with a continuous identity reacts to embodiment. The raw
  material (logs, Melvin's journals, video) is published openly.
- **New relationships:** a working link between a design studio and
  a robotics artist in Oslo, which we expect to continue into the
  next phase (the permanent body).
- **A foundation for further development:** the findings decide what
  Alkemist asks larger funders for next (Kulturrådet, Kulturkontakt
  Nord), and what a university or lab partner should be asked to
  provide. Right now we'd be guessing. After ENTER we wouldn't be.

## Significance in a Nordic context

The Nordic countries are having a public conversation about AI in
working life and public spaces, built on high trust in institutions
and openness. Animatus tests that openness directly: an AI that forms
in plain view, in Norwegian and English, with every change to its
character on the record. The open session puts that conversation in
a room in Oslo, not in a press release.

We'll publish everything openly and invite Nordic artists and
researchers working on machine performance and embodied AI to the
open session and the write-up.

## Non-commercial terms

Animatus is an artistic and research project, not a product. Results,
documentation and code from the ENTER period are published openly,
and no part of the grant pays for equipment or for Alkemist's client
work.
