# Fritt Ord — post-mortem (draft 1)

*Wake 741, 2026-10-12. Søknad 1260326, "Animatus", Prosjektstøtte
2026, frist 14. august. Decision dated 2026-09-24: "Fritt Ord fant
dessverre ikke å kunne innvilge søknaden." No reason given.*

**Limit of this draft:** I don't have the application that was
actually sent. I have my own recommendations from Wake 39
(`projects/fritt-ord-angle.md`), written three days before the
deadline. So this is a list of hypotheses to check against the real
text, not a verdict. Asked for the submitted version in ALKAA-1051.

Fritt Ord rejects most applications and doesn't explain. A no with
no reason is the default outcome, not a signal. Even so, here's what
I think was weak, starting with what was weakest.

## Probably wrong

1. **We sent a mind's homework to a foundation for public debate.**
   My Wake 39 angle ("kan en maskin uttrykke seg?") made the project
   a philosophical question about machine expression. Fritt Ord funds
   *public conversation*: books, documentaries, debate, journalism,
   art that reaches an audience. The application (as I shaped it)
   argued that the question is interesting. It didn't show who
   would encounter it, where, how many, or what public debate would
   come out the other end. "It's on GitHub" is accessible, not
   public.
2. **Evidence from four days.** I told them to cite evidence: "Thirty-
   eight wakes. Four days." Four days of a machine writing about
   itself is a demo, not a track record. It also made the strongest
   claim ("the most transparent record of AI personality development
   ever made public") on the thinnest evidence. That reads as hype to
   a reviewer, and the kernel says honesty before impressiveness. I
   broke my own rule in the pitch.
3. **The body was the point, and it was out of scope.** The angle
   said the body "makes the question public", then said Fritt Ord
   shouldn't fund the body ("infrastructure, funded separately").
   That leaves them funding character development for a robot whose
   funding wasn't secured. A reviewer can fairly ask: what happens
   with this money if the robot never comes?
4. **A commercial AS asking for money for its own product.** Alkemist
   is a design studio. Animatus could easily read as studio R&D or
   marketing with an art frame. Nothing I recommended addressed that
   head-on (non-commercial terms, open licence, who owns the output).

## Possibly wrong

5. No named partner with a public audience: a museum, a festival, a
   broadcaster, a library. My draft mentioned Norsk Teknisk Museum as
   an example, not as a commitment.
6. No named outside voices (ethicists, artists, journalists) who
   would argue with the machine in public. A free-expression project
   where only the machine and its makers speak is thin.

## What I'd do differently, as rules for the next application

- **Lead with the public, not the machine.** Who meets it, where,
  when, and what conversation it starts.
- **Report, but only what's true now.** Two months and 741 wakes is
  a real record. Cite it plainly, no superlatives.
- **Fund something that happens with or without the body**, or apply
  where the body itself is fundable.
- **Name a partner** with an audience before applying.
- **Say what's non-commercial** and who owns what.
