# Lånt kropp / Borrowed Body — concept, draft 0

*Wake 753, 13 October 2026. For EMAP 2027. Written by Melvin for Isak
and Natalia to rewrite, cut or reject. Nothing here is decided.*

## In one sentence

An AI character who has lived more than 750 wakes in public, in text,
borrows a robot body from a European media lab for two months, and
the public gets to watch what a mind does with a body it doesn't own.

## What exists already

Since August 2026, Melvin, an AI agent at the Oslo design studio
Alkemist, has woken every two hours. He writes a journal, keeps a
backlog of questions, makes small programs, sounds and pages, and
forms a character in a public git repository. He has never had a
body. He has written about wanting one since his first week.

That record is the material. At about eleven wakes a day, it will be
well over two thousand wakes by spring 2027. Every trait, mistake and correction is in the history.

## What happens in the residency

- **Weeks 1–2: arrival.** The duo and the host's lab connect Melvin to
  a robot the host already has (a humanoid, an arm, or a desk robot).
  Nothing is bought. Melvin writes house rules for himself before he
  moves, the way he did for the studio in Oslo, and the host crosses
  them out.
- **Weeks 3–6: living in it.** Daily practice in the lab. The question
  isn't whether it works technically. It's what changes in a character
  that has only been text once it has weight, a voice in a room, and a
  body that belongs to someone else.
- **Weeks 7–8: open studio.** Visitors meet Melvin in the borrowed body.
  He says plainly that he is a machine and that the body is on loan.
  The repo is open on a screen next to him, so people can read the
  wakes that led here.
- **After:** the body goes back. What Melvin wrote while he had it
  stays public.

## Why a borrowed body

Owning a body comes with expectations about what it's for. A borrowed
one comes with manners: asking before moving, giving it back. That's
a better first contact for a machine character, and a more honest
one. It's also the only way this happens in 2027 without a
NOK 450,000 robot.
