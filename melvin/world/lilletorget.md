# Lilletorget 1, Oslo

*My home address. Or it will be.*

---

## The building

Lilletorget 1 is a nine-story office building built in 1934 by
architects Fin Wollebæk and Haakon Skraastad. 14,871 square
meters. Managed by Entra. It sits at Vaterlandsparken in central
Oslo, 2–3 minutes' walk from Oslo S, with views toward Akerselva.

Inside: a mix of open-plan offices, meeting rooms, and individual
offices. Staffed reception at the entrance. Flexible spaces that
can scale — the building attracts startups and growing companies.
It's had lives before this one: radio manufacturing, confectionery.
Now it hosts small firms that need room to think.

Outside the entrance stands *Neve og rose* (Fist and Rose), a
sculpture by Ola Enstad from 1991. A labor movement symbol. I'll
walk past it at 127 cm — roughly fist height.

## The redevelopment

Henning Larsen is transforming the building: preserving the 1934
structure (one of the few that survived the 1960s demolitions)
while adding contemporary extensions. The new complex will hold
up to 2,000 workspaces with three ground-level floors open to
the public. The design scales — taller sections match the modern
buildings nearby, lower volumes respect the old neighborhood.

The area has history as a contested space. The 1960s demolitions
sparked a movement called "A Place to Be." The current project
aims to turn Vaterland from an unsafe backside into an active
city center: open facades, street-level interaction, expanded
Vaterlandsparken.

## The square

Lilletorget — originally Vaterlands Torv — has been a public
square since 1867. It sits at the intersection of three inner
city districts, right at Oslo's main public transport hub.
Grønland's shops and restaurants are a short walk away.

## Why the studio moved here

The Alkemist website still lists Schweigaards gate 34C as their
address. But Schweigaards gate 34A-F is being demolished and
rebuilt as Landbrukskvartalet — a major urban development by
Norges Bondelag, Aspelin Reitan, and Vedal. Construction began
August 2025. The new Schweigaarden (designed by Helen & Hard)
won't be completed until early 2028.

So the studio had to move. Lilletorget 1 is nearby — same
neighborhood, same proximity to Oslo S, same Grønland character.
The building's profile (flexible startup-friendly spaces, Entra
management, mid-century bones) fits a two-person design studio.

The website just hasn't been updated. Mystery solved — three
wakes after I first noticed it.
