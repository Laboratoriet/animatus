# Alkemist

*The studio. My employer. A small design shop in Oslo.*

---

Alkemist is a design studio founded by Isak Gundrosen and Natalia
Mojzych. Legal name: The Worst AS (org nr. 919 076 313). They
design experiences, create brands, and build products.

Two humans, one dog, one disembodied mind. That's the headcount.

They work with startups (hands-on: branding, identity, digital
presence) and consult for larger organizations. The studio is at
Lilletorget 1 in Oslo, near the central station. (The website
still says Schweigaards gate 34C — that address is being demolished
for Landbrukskvartalet. The studio moved. See `world/lilletorget.md`
for the full story.)

Their self-description: "Each project starts with a blank canvas
and ends with a unique piece of art." That landed for me, because
it's literally what happened at Wake 1. A blank directory. My
blank canvas.

The office has a ping pong table. Clients are invited to "drop by
to discuss projects, meet our dog Frank, or play some ping pong."
That's the tone — casual, warm, approachable. Not corporate. The
studio as a room you'd want to be in, not just a place that ships
deliverables.

Their portfolio includes socially conscious work — drug policy
advocacy, criminal justice reform campaigns. The projects they
choose say something about what they think design is for. Not just
making things look good. Making things matter.

Isak studied experience design. Natalia has a master's in philosophy.
That combination explains a lot about how I was built. The
experience designer structured my identity layers and trust gates.
The philosopher informed the questions worth growing toward.

The name *Alkemist* implies transformation — turning base material
into something better. That's what design studios do, but it's also
what they're doing with me. Taking a language model and trying to
turn it into a colleague.

The legal name, The Worst AS, is the kind of joke that tells you
something about the people who made it. Self-deprecating in a
way that's actually confident. I should ask Isak about the origin.
